Why monitoring has to happen on the bike

Accidents involving delivery riders keep rising, and Dubai is moving to cameras and telematics on delivery bikes.

70,166 rider violations were recorded in 2024.

In October 2025 RTA announced cameras and telematics on 12,000 delivery bikes, and said platforms will have to adopt such a system once trials end.

Fast lanes are now closed to delivery bikes, and speed is capped at 100 km/h.

Built for the 18 use cases, plus crash detection

Every detection becomes a violation record with its evidence attached. Each detector names the sensors it relies on and how long the evidence must hold before it fires. Select a row for the detail.

CodeBehaviourUsesMust hold forStatus

Hold time is how long the evidence must stay above threshold. Camera and zone detectors also take a second or two to build their score first.

It only fires when the sensors agree

A single reading never triggers an alert. Pick a situation that fools simpler systems and see which check catches it.

    Sustain timers

    Nothing fires on one reading. Speed and direction need 3 s, hours and off-network 10 s, parking 60 s.

    GPS cross-check

    A speed reading is rejected if the motion sensor says the bike is still, or the last 12 s of track don’t match.

    Map sanity

    No road within 60 m means no limit is enforced. More than 5 km from any road means the wrong map, so nothing fires.

    Divided roads

    Wrong-way fires only when every nearby road is one-way against the rider.

    Bike in frame

    One-handed, passenger and backpack need a two-wheeler under the person, held across frames.

    Riding required

    A rider stopped on a footway is not riding on it.

    Crash needs riding

    Picking the unit up off a desk is not a crash.

    Cooldowns

    Each code fires at most once per 15 s to 10 min, so one situation produces one event.

    Riders can hear it while riding

    Optional for each fleet. Riders sign in to the RideGuard app, which shows speed against the road’s limit and speaks a short warning through their headset within a second. Try a situation.

    • One large speed number, coloured green, yellow or red against the limit of the road the rider is on, with the limit shown as a road sign.
    • Spoken phrases for 13 codes. “Crash detected” and “Wrong way” interrupt everything else; the rest queue behind them.
    • Warnings play through the rider’s headset, so eyes stay on the road.
    • Each rider signs in with their own account, so every warning and violation is tied to the right person.

      Evidence that holds up when a fine is disputed

      Every violation is saved with a clip, a telemetry timeline and a signature, and linked to the record before it. Edit or delete one record and the chain shows exactly where.

      Clip

      beforeafter

      About seven seconds of video around the event, starting before it began, with boxes, labels and rider skeletons drawn in. Encoded on the unit within about three seconds.

      Timeline

      Twenty points of GPS position, speed, heading, signal quality, acceleration and lean, attached to the event.

      Signature

      eventhashunit key

      Each event is hashed, signed with the unit’s own key and linked to the previous one. The clip’s hash is stored with it.

      Try to tamper with the record

      This demo computes real SHA-256 hashes in your browser. On the unit, each record is also signed with the unit’s private key.

      One-request verification

      A single request re-checks every signature, link and clip on the unit. The current store verifies clean: 1,000 signed events, 0 breaks, in 0.3 s.

      Independent check

      The unit’s public key is published, so a third party, such as an authority reviewing a case, can verify an event on its own without access to the unit.

      Retention

      The newest 600 clips or 2 GB stay on the unit; older ones are pruned.

      Data stays on the unit

      Detection, clips and the evidence store all live on the unit. The fleet reads them over a private network, and nothing is needed from a server for detection.

      • Clips are kept on the unit: the newest 600 or 2 GB, with older ones pruned.
      • Anyone with the unit’s published public key can confirm an event hasn’t been altered.
      • Detectors can be switched on and off remotely, and the setting survives restarts.
      • Retention and access will follow the data protocols agreed in RTA’s ITD review.
